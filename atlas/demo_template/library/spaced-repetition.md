## Overview

Spaced repetition is a way of studying where you go back over material at longer and longer gaps instead of all at once. For example, you might review a new fact after one day, then after three days, then after a week, then after a few weeks. The gap gets longer each time you remember the fact correctly.

## Why spacing beats cramming

Cramming means doing many repetitions in a single session. It can feel productive and often works for a test the next morning, but what you learn that way fades quickly. If you spread the same number of reviews over days or weeks, you remember much more in the long run. Researchers call this the **spacing effect**, and the source describes it as one of the most reliable findings in memory research.

One explanation is that a review helps most when the memory has started to fade but is not yet gone. Recalling a half-forgotten fact takes effort, and that effort strengthens the memory more than re-reading something you already know well.

## Flashcards and scheduling

Many people do spaced repetition with flashcards:

- **Software flashcard apps** schedule each card on its own. Cards you answer easily come back after a long gap, and cards you miss come back soon.
- **Paper systems** such as the **Leitner box** do the same thing by hand. Cards move between boxes, and each box is reviewed at a different frequency.

## Common mistakes

- **Making cards too big.** A card that asks for a whole paragraph is hard to grade honestly. Small cards that each hold one idea work better.
- **Memorizing without understanding.** Spacing helps you keep what you have learned, but it does not create understanding by itself.
- **Skipping days.** The schedule depends on regular reviews. Long breaks build up large backlogs that are discouraging to clear.

## Pairing with active recall

Spaced repetition works best alongside **active recall**, which means trying to produce the answer yourself before you look at it.
