## Overview

A habit is a behavior that has become automatic. A situation triggers it, not a conscious decision. Brushing your teeth before bed and checking your phone when you sit down are everyday examples.

## The Habit Loop

Habits are commonly described as a loop with three parts:

1. **Cue**: a trigger in the environment or routine, such as a time of day, a place, or a feeling.
2. **Routine**: the behavior itself.
3. **Reward**: something satisfying that follows, which makes the brain more likely to repeat the loop.

When the behavior is repeated in a stable context, the link between cue and routine gets stronger until the behavior happens with little thought.

## How Long Habit Formation Takes

The popular claim that habits take 21 days to form is not supported by research. In a frequently cited study, Phillippa Lally and colleagues found that participants needed anywhere from a few weeks to many months before a new daily behavior felt automatic. The time varied a lot between people and between behaviors. Missing a single day did not seem to derail the process.

## Building and Breaking Habits

- **Attach new habits to existing cues.** Tie a new behavior to a reliable trigger you already have, e.g. "After I pour my morning coffee, I will review my flashcards."
- **Start very small.** A tiny version of the behavior is easier to repeat consistently, and consistency is what builds automaticity.
- **Change the environment.** Removing a cue, such as keeping your phone in another room, often works better than relying on willpower.

## Habits and Learning

Study methods like spaced repetition only work if reviews actually happen regularly. Making review a habit tied to a daily cue is often what separates people who stick with a learning plan from those who don't.
