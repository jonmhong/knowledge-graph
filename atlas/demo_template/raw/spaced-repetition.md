# Spaced repetition: reviewing just before you forget

Spaced repetition is a study method where you review material at gradually increasing intervals instead of all at once. You might review a new fact after one day, then after three days, then a week, then a few weeks, stretching the gap each time you remember it successfully.

## Why spacing beats cramming

Cramming, meaning many repetitions packed into one session, can feel productive and often works for a test the next morning. But what is learned that way fades quickly. Spreading the same number of reviews over days or weeks leads to much better long-term retention. Researchers call this the spacing effect, and it is one of the most reliable findings in the study of memory.

One explanation is that a review is most useful when the memory has started to fade but has not yet disappeared. Pulling back a half-forgotten fact takes effort, and that effort strengthens the memory more than re-reading something you already know well.

## Flashcards and algorithms

Many people practice spaced repetition with flashcards. Software flashcard apps schedule each card individually: cards you answer easily come back after a long gap, and cards you miss come back soon. Paper systems such as the Leitner box do the same thing by moving cards between boxes that are reviewed at different frequencies.

## Common mistakes

- **Making cards too big.** A card that asks for a whole paragraph is hard to grade honestly. Small, single-idea cards work better.
- **Memorizing without understanding.** Spacing helps you keep what you learned, but it does not create understanding on its own.
- **Skipping days.** The schedule depends on regular reviews. Long breaks create large backlogs that are discouraging to clear.

Spaced repetition works best when combined with active recall, where you try to produce the answer before looking at it.
