# The generation effect: remembering what you produce yourself

The generation effect is the finding that people remember information better when they generate it themselves than when they simply read it. In a classic version of the experiment, one group reads word pairs such as "hot – cold", while another group sees "hot – c___" and has to fill in the word. The group that fills in the blanks remembers more of the words afterwards.

## Why generating helps

Producing an answer forces you to search memory, make connections and commit to a response. That extra processing creates a richer memory trace than passively reading the same information. It works even when the generation is easy, and even when people briefly get the answer wrong before being corrected.

## Errors can help

It might seem that guessing before you know the answer would plant wrong information. Studies suggest the opposite is often true: attempting an answer and then seeing the correct one usually leads to better memory than just being shown the answer, as long as feedback follows soon. The attempt prepares the mind to notice and hold on to the correction.

## Elaboration

A close relative of generation is elaboration: explaining an idea in your own words, asking "why is this true?", or connecting it to an example from your own life. Elaboration links new information to what you already know, which, as research on forgetting suggests, makes it far more durable than isolated facts.

## Using it

- Predict the answer before reading the explanation.
- Rewrite key points in your own words instead of highlighting them.
- Make up your own examples of a new concept.
- After a lecture or article, write the main ideas from memory before reviewing your notes.
