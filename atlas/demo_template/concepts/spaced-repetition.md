---
title: Spaced Repetition
---

Spaced repetition is a way of studying in which you review the same material several times, waiting longer between each review instead of going over it all at once. It helps you remember things for months or years rather than just until tomorrow. A common schedule reviews a new fact after one day, then three days, then a week, then a few weeks. The gap grows each time you recall the fact correctly ([[library/spaced-repetition|Spaced Repetition: Reviewing Just Before You Forget]]).

## Why spacing beats cramming

Cramming means doing many repetitions in a single session. It can feel productive and often works for a test the next morning, but what you learn that way fades quickly. Spreading the same number of reviews over days or weeks leads to much better long-term memory. Researchers call this the **spacing effect**, and it is described as one of the most reliable findings in memory research ([[library/spaced-repetition|Spaced Repetition: Reviewing Just Before You Forget]]).

One explanation is that a review helps most when the memory has started to fade but is not yet gone. Recalling a half-forgotten fact takes effort. That effort strengthens the memory more than re-reading something you already know well. This is why spaced repetition is often summed up as "reviewing just before you forget."

## Flashcards and scheduling

Many people do spaced repetition with flashcards ([[library/spaced-repetition|Spaced Repetition: Reviewing Just Before You Forget]]):

- **Software flashcard apps** schedule each card on its own. Cards you answer easily come back after a long gap, and cards you miss come back soon.
- **Paper systems** such as the **Leitner box** do the same thing by hand. Cards move between boxes, and each box is reviewed at a different frequency.

## Common mistakes

According to [[library/spaced-repetition|Spaced Repetition: Reviewing Just Before You Forget]], the usual mistakes are:

- **Making cards too big.** A card that asks for a whole paragraph is hard to grade honestly. Small cards that each hold one idea work better.
- **Memorizing without understanding.** Spacing helps you keep what you have learned, but it does not create understanding on its own.
- **Skipping days.** The schedule depends on regular reviews. Long breaks build up large backlogs that are discouraging to clear.

## Pairing with active recall

Spaced repetition works best alongside **active recall**, which means trying to produce the answer yourself before you look at it ([[library/spaced-repetition|Spaced Repetition: Reviewing Just Before You Forget]]). Spacing decides *when* you review, and active recall decides *how* you review. Each one makes the other more effective.
