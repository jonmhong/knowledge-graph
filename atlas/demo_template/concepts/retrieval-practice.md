title: Retrieval Practice

Retrieval practice is a way of studying in which you pull information out of your own memory, for example by answering questions or writing down what you remember, instead of reading the material again. Most people think of tests as a way to check whether they have learned something. Research in cognitive psychology suggests that the act of remembering is also one of the most effective ways to learn in the first place [[library/retrieval-practice|Retrieval Practice: Testing as a Way to Learn]].

## Core Idea

Retrieval practice means deliberately recalling information from memory instead of taking it in again by re-reading. Common forms include answering practice questions, using flashcards, and writing down everything you can remember about a topic [[library/retrieval-practice|Retrieval Practice: Testing as a Way to Learn]].

## The Testing Effect

Psychologists Henry Roediger and Jeffrey Karpicke ran a well-known series of studies comparing two groups of students:

- One group **re-read** a passage several times.
- The other group **read it once, then practiced recalling** it.

Right after studying, the re-readers often did about as well as the recall group, or better. Days later, though, the students who had practiced recall remembered considerably more. This long-term advantage of recalling over re-reading is called the **testing effect** [[library/retrieval-practice|Retrieval Practice: Testing as a Way to Learn]].

## Why Re-reading Feels Better Than It Is

Re-reading makes the text feel familiar, and something that looks familiar seems learned. But *recognizing* information that is in front of you is very different from *producing* it from memory. Retrieval practice is harder and less pleasant, which is part of why people underuse it. That same difficulty is also what makes it work [[library/retrieval-practice|Retrieval Practice: Testing as a Way to Learn]].

## Practical Techniques

- Close the book, write a summary from memory, then check what you missed.
- Turn headings into questions and answer them before reading each section.
- Use flashcards, and say the answer out loud before flipping the card.
- Explain the idea to someone else without notes.

These techniques come from [[library/retrieval-practice|Retrieval Practice: Testing as a Way to Learn]].

## Relationship to Spaced Repetition

Retrieval practice pairs naturally with spaced repetition. Spacing decides **when** to review, and retrieval decides **how** to review [[library/retrieval-practice|Retrieval Practice: Testing as a Way to Learn]].
